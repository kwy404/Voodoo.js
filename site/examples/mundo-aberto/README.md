# Open world

A small, tightly packed 3D town, generated by code, with a day and night cycle,
shadows, fog and traffic. Pure WebGL2: no Three.js, no Babylon, no CDN. Voodoo
has zero runtime dependencies and the examples respect that.

Open it at `http://localhost:5173/examples/mundo-aberto/`.

## The split this demo exists to prove

There are two files, and the boundary between them is the subject:

| File | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| **`mundo.js`** | Pure WebGL2. Terrain, city, shadows, sky, physics, traffic. It does not know about Voodoo, it does not touch the interface DOM, it does not know what a slider is. |
| **`index.html`** | Declarative Voodoo. HUD, speedometer, clock, minimap, settings panel, pause menu, touch controls. It does not write a single line of GL. |

The entire bridge fits into three things:

- `motor.opcoes`, an object of plain values that the interface writes to;
- `motor.controle`, which the touch buttons push;
- `motor.aoAtualizar(fn)`, a callback that hands numbers back to a store.

**No reactivity crosses the render loop.** Reading a proxy sixty times a second
would be a waste: the engine notifies the interface ten times a second, and the
interface writes to the engine only when someone moves something.

## What Voodoo controls

**Three components with `V.component`.** `relogio-mundo` only formats the time
and the phase of the day; `velocimetro` draws an SVG arc and a number;
`mundo-aberto` holds the rest. The first two do not know that a 3D engine
exists: they read the store and return markup.

**One global store with `V.store`.** `$store.jogo` holds the current screen, the
FPS, the speed, the time, the position, the distance covered and the speed
record. The HUD reads all of it straight from the HTML with
`{ $store.jogo.fps }`, and no component has to talk to another one for that.

**Preferences in `V.storage`.** Time, fog, view distance, sun intensity,
quality, camera and the traffic toggle come back the way you left them on your
last visit. Tampered values in storage fall back to the default, field by field.

**Every slider changes the world in the same frame.** `v-model.number` writes to
the component state and the `@input` copies it into `motor.opcoes`. There is no
apply button.

**The lists are `v-for`.** The quality levels, the camera modes, the speedometer
ticks and even the decorative skyline on the "no WebGL2" screen all come from
arrays.

**The screens are `v-show` over a store.** Start screen, game and pause are
three blocks watching `$store.jogo.tela`.

## Rendering techniques

**Terrain as a single mesh.** Value noise written by hand, with a fixed seed,
sampled on a 168x168 grid (about 56 thousand triangles). The streets follow the
relief instead of being flattened: it gives the air of a hillside town and it
comes for free, because it is one single mesh, with no cutting. The same
`altura(x, z)` function answers "how high is it here?" for the car and for the
traffic, so nothing floats or sinks.

**Streets drawn in the fragment shader.** The city mesh is periodic, so asphalt,
sidewalk, curb, dashed center line, edge line and crosswalk all come out of two
`mod` functions over the world coordinate. No geometry, no texture.

**Instancing for everything with volume.** Buildings, lamp posts, trunks, cars
and pedestrians are the same unit box, drawn with
`drawElementsInstanced`. Each instance carries position, size, color, a 3x3
rotation matrix and a `(tipo, semente)` pair. The tree canopies use a low
polygon sphere, on the same scheme. That is **four draw calls per frame**: sky,
terrain, static instances, dynamic instances.

**Windows procedurally, in the shader.** The instance's `tipo` switches on a
grid of windows computed in local coordinates in meters, with a frame, a slab
between floors, a parapet at the top and a different ground floor band. Which
windows are lit comes out of a hash over `(coluna, andar, semente)`, so the same
building always lights the same ones.

**Directional shadow map.** A `DEPTH_COMPONENT24` texture with
`COMPARE_REF_TO_TEXTURE`, sampled with `sampler2DShadow` and 3x3 PCF. The light
volume is orthographic and follows the car. On top of that, the base of each
building gets a **fake contact occlusion** (a `smoothstep` darkening over the
first few meters): even with the shadow turned off on low quality, the volume
stays anchored to the ground.

**Sky in a full screen triangle.** A gradient by height, the discs of the sun
and the moon, hash stars that only show up at night, and fbm clouds projected
onto a high plane. The `corCeu(dir)` function is the same one the fog uses, so
the horizon and the fog are literally the same color and the end of the world
disappears without a seam.

**Fog with a cubic falloff.** `1 - exp(-(d * k)^3)`: the near field stays clean
and the world closes in quickly at the far end. With exponent 2 the middle of
the street already came out washed out. The `k` is derived from the view
distance, so the two sliders never fight each other.

**Lighting.** A directional sun with color and strength coming from a palette of
ten keys per hour, desaturated hemispheric ambient (the sky colors are too
saturated to serve as diffuse light directly), Blinn-Phong specular, a sky
reflection by Fresnel at a very grazing angle, and a headlight cone that lights
terrain and buildings. The street lamps are not real lights: the pool of light
is computed by `mod` on the same coordinates where the lamps were planted. It
closes with filmic ACES and gamma.

**Day and night cycle.** A palette of ten keys interpolated by hour controls
zenith, horizon, sun color and strength, ambient and exposure. Below the horizon
the main light becomes the moon. The emissives (windows, headlights, tail
lights, lamps) are divided by the exposure, otherwise they would saturate to
pure white at the high exposure of night.

## Performance

Measured in Chrome, WebGL2, in the browser on this machine:

| Scenario | Cost per frame |
| --- | --- |
| High quality, 1280x720 | ~0.8 ms |
| High quality, 1920x1080 | ~1.2 ms |
| Low quality, 1920x1080 | ~1.1 ms |

The budget for 60 FPS is 16.7 ms, so the demo runs **locked at 60 FPS by
vsync**, with a wide margin. The HUD shows the real FPS measured by the loop
itself.

The measurement was made by queueing frames and forcing synchronization with
`readPixels`. `gl.finish()` is no good: in Chrome it returns before the GPU has
finished.

The world has **199 buildings, 295 trees and 264 lamp posts**, about 1500
instances per frame counting the traffic, and 56 thousand terrain triangles.

## What was cut, and why

- **A single shadow cascade.** The light volume follows the car and covers
  210 meters. Tall buildings with a low sun cast a shadow longer than that, and
  the shadow is cut off at the end. A second cascade would double the depth pass
  and was not worth the cost for a world this size.
- **No culling.** The static instances go to the GPU in full every frame. With
  1500 boxes that is cheaper than rebuilding the buffer by frustum every frame;
  in a world ten times larger the math would flip.
- **Simplified collision.** There is no per building test. Since the city is a
  grid, the car is only kept from entering the middle of the block, and scraping
  costs speed in proportion to the jolt. It is cheap, it is always right and it
  never lets the car go through a wall, but it also does not hit a lamp post or
  a tree.
- **Traffic with no collision between cars.** The cars pass through each other
  and through the player. They exist to make the world look alive, not to
  simulate traffic.
- **One headlight cone, not two.** Two lights would look like two lights. A
  single wider one costs half as much and nobody notices.
- **Lamp posts only on the avenues.** The cross streets have no lamps. That
  halves the number of instances, and the contrast between a lit avenue and a
  dark street ended up being more interesting than the symmetrical version.
- **No configurable antialiasing.** The context MSAA is fixed at creation.
  Quality changes resolution (maximum `devicePixelRatio`), shadow map size and
  view distance.

## Controls

| Action | Keyboard | Touch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accelerate / reverse | `W` `S` or arrows | pedals on the right |
| Steer | `A` `D` or arrows | pedals on the left |
| Turbo | `Shift` | none |
| Brake | `Space` | none |
| Switch camera | `C` | settings panel |
| Hide minimap and speedometer | `H` | settings panel |
| Pause | `Esc` | Pause button |

The touch controls appear on their own on a coarse pointer or on a screen up to
860 px.

## Behavior details

- The loop pauses on `visibilitychange` and comes back when the tab reappears.
- On unmount, the component removes every listener, deletes programs, buffers,
  VAOs, texture and framebuffer, and releases the context.
- The interface animations respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. The world keeps
  moving: it is the content, not an ornament.
- Without WebGL2, the demo shows a screen explaining why, with a skyline drawn
  in CSS. Everything else on the page keeps working.
- If the `V.gpu` module exists, this demo does not depend on it: the WebGL2 path
  here is self-sufficient on purpose.
